Inside the optimizer The problem CVXPY solves, and two checks you can run from your current settings

1The problem

Variables (for each time step k)
\(\mathbf r_k, \mathbf v_k \in \mathbb R^3\) position and velocity, \(\mathbf u_k \in \mathbb R^3\) thrust acceleration, \(\Gamma_k\) slack (thrust size), 50 steps of \(\Delta t = t_f / N\).
Objective
\(\displaystyle \min \; \Delta t \sum_k \Gamma_k\)  ≈ total Δv, which sets the fuel burned
Constraints
  • \(\mathbf r_{k+1}, \mathbf v_{k+1}\) from \(\mathbf u_k + \mathbf g\) (dynamics)linear
  • start at \(\mathbf r_0, \mathbf v_0\); end at the gate, 15 m above the padlinear
  • \(\|\mathbf u_k\| \le \Gamma_k \le a_{\max}\) = 21.3 m/s²cone
  • \(\|\mathbf u_k\| \ge a_{\min}\) = 7.5 m/s² (engine can't throttle lower)non-convex
  • replaced by \(\Gamma_k \ge a_{\min}\)relaxed, convex
  • \(u_{z,k} \ge \Gamma_k \cos\theta_{\max}\), tilt ≤ 35°linear
  • \(z_k \ge \tan\gamma \,\|(x_k, y_k)\|\), glide slope 20°cone
\(a_{\max}\) is 85 % of engine power (the rest is a reserve for in-flight corrections). Flight time \(t_f\) is searched outside the convex problem.

2Why the convexification matters

Straight down (1D) the floor is just an interval, which is convex. The hole only appears once the thrust can point in different directions (2D/3D). Below: the same landing solved two ways from your current settings.

3-DoF Mathematical Derivation, Lossless Convexification & How It's Checked

1. What is 3-DoF in Powered Descent Guidance?

In aerospace trajectory optimization, 3-DoF (3 Degrees of Freedom) represents the full 3D spatial translation of a point-mass vehicle:

3D State Vectors: $$ \mathbf{r}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} x(t) \\ y(t) \\ z(t) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \mathbf{v}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} v_x(t) \\ v_y(t) \\ v_z(t) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \mathbf{u}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} u_x(t) \\ u_y(t) \\ u_z(t) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3 $$ where $x$ is downrange, $y$ is crossrange, and $z$ is altitude above the landing pad.

(Note: 6-DoF introduces the 3 rotational degrees of freedom: roll, pitch, yaw and angular rates $\boldsymbol{\omega}$. In modern guidance, 3-DoF guidance generates the fuel-optimal acceleration command $\mathbf{u}^*(t)$, and an inner-loop attitude controller commands engine gimbals and RCS to point the vehicle accordingly.)

2. 3D Second-Order Cone Constraints & Lossless Convexification

The minimum engine throttle constraint $0 < u_{\min} \le \|\mathbf{u}(t)\|$ creates a non-convex spherical shell. Açıkmeşe & Ploen (2007) relaxed this via the slack variable $\Gamma(t)$:

  • 3D Second-Order Cone (SOC): $$ \sqrt{u_x(t)^2 + u_y(t)^2 + u_z(t)^2} \le \Gamma(t) $$
  • Thrust Magnitude Bounds: $$ u_{\min} \le \Gamma(t) \le u_{\max} $$
  • 3D Thrust Pointing Cone (Gimbal Limit): $$ u_z(t) \ge \Gamma(t) \cos \theta_{\max} $$
  • 3D Glide-Slope Terrain Cone: $$ z(t) \ge \sqrt{x(t)^2 + y(t)^2} \tan \gamma_{\text{gs}} $$
  • Objective: $$ \min \int_0^{t_f} \Gamma(t) \, dt $$

3. How the results are checked

Two checks run CVXPY live in your browser, from whatever sliders you have set. They're at the bottom of the page under Inside the optimizer. The numbers below were measured at the default settings (500 m up, falling at 40 m/s, engine 25 m/s², lowest throttle 30 %, tilt ≤ 35°).

A limit we found: the lossless guarantee only holds where the original problem is feasible. During the flight-time search, CVXPY first returned a flight 0.23 s shorter than physically possible, with $\Gamma = 7.5$ but $\|\mathbf u\| = 6.14$ m/s², below the engine minimum. The solver now rejects any answer whose slack isn't tight ($\max|\Gamma - \|\mathbf u\|| > 10^{-4} a_{\max}$).
